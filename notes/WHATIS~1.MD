**What is the "rule of instrument," and how does it apply to problem-solving with data structures?**
The "rule of instrument," also known as Maslow's Hammer, is a cognitive bias that involves an over-reliance on a familiar tool or method, regardless of its appropriateness for the task at hand. The concept is often summarized by the phrase, "If all you have is a hammer, everything looks like a nail." In data structures, this means carefully selecting a structure tailored to the problem instead of defaulting to one you are most comfortable with, ensuring flexibility and precision in solutions. A common example amongst novice programmer is the overuse of lists or arrays, where a stack or a queue would be more appropriate.

**How does the choice of a data structure impact the efficiency of a solution?**
The right data structure can significantly enhance the efficiency of data manipulation and retrieval, reducing computational complexity. For example, using a hash table to store user data allows constant-time lookups, making systems like Facebook's search feature quick and scalable.
